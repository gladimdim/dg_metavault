# [05:44-10:00 Why Recruiters](https://youtu.be/F76jxcjHq_s?t=344)

![screen1.png](../assets/review_dou_2/screen1.png)

Everyone who asks "why do we even need recruiters" has either never hired anyone themselves, or hired very few. Once you start hiring a lot, it becomes your job and you turn into a recruiter yourself lol.

I've had the chance to work with excellent recruiters on both sides, and they were always very much to the point. If someone thinks he's such a diamond that the CTO or VP of Eng should be headhunting him personally, you're mistaken.
Because there's so much junk on the market that you'd just hang yourself sorting through it all.

The main whims of candidates:

- They check your English? Well, yes, there are characters who after 10 years in the outsourcing galleys can't put two words together and then just hem and haw. By the way, among the new generation this almost never happens, their English is at a pretty high level! Which can't help but make me happy :)

- They ask for clarification on some positions in your CV? If the team is interested in specific technologies or experience, they ask recruiters to narrow the search filters. If experience with a particular technology is really important on the project, then yes, the recruiter will grill you about it.
Especially since technically they're not testing you, but they can ask questions to add more information to your profile.

So why show off like that, let alone look down on recruiters - I don't understand, just like the podcast hosts don't. Forgot 2008, when people were crawling on their knees after whole offices of cattle farms got washed up on the shore?
When 300-400 Java devs were landing on the beach every day. I know of cases when recruiters were even paid just to be considered. And now look at that, they asked about English... ew..

# [15:19 GitLab hid its salary calculator / Companies keep salaries hush-hush](https://youtu.be/F76jxcjHq_s?t=919)

![screen3.png](../assets/review_dou_2/screen3.png)

Why do companies forbid colleagues from sharing their salaries with each other? Because there's always a sucker who gets paid 0.5x but does 3x the work for everyone. Always. So for the company, keeping such facts quiet is a very convenient option, so that this sucker doesn't leave and keeps working. You can also throw him +0.2x a year later and the sucker will work even harder.

It's also a manipulation technique used by management. For example, you tell one Full Time Equivalent that he got the biggest bonus (130%), so he's the coolest in the company. Then the same manager tells another one: you got the biggest bonus on the team, a whole 115%!! I fought for it personally with the CFO, put my career on the line for your raise!! Then he tells the third one: you got... Well, you get the algorithm.

If you want to find out whether you're a sucker or not, go to some interviews, you'll learn everything there :)

# [37:02 About stock](https://youtu.be/F76jxcjHq_s?t=2220)

![screen5.png](../assets/review_dou_2/screen5.png)

All this stock and its holders remind me of Soviet folks who had 20,000 - 40,000 rubles (that's about 4-6 cars) in the state savings bank, dreaming about the future and how they'd use all that money someday. Except then the end came and it all vanished. Same with these pieces of paper: you have virtual money that you might somehow use someday, if this tech bubble doesn't burst and leave you with nothing.

I read a tweet by a Googler who joined the company in 2011. He showed a screenshot: he has 1 million dollars in stock! Everyone went crazy, of course, well done, now that's success! And I remembered my godfather, who started importing computers in the 90s and made insane margins on it.
He kept about 40k bucks somewhere, which for the 90s was simply cosmic money. Except he never spent it, and what's 40k now? A new car from the dealership. You can't even buy a house. But he was smoldering over that money, just like this Googler with his 1 mil. Mind you, you'll pay at least 50% tax on it, and in advance too.
That is, to cash out 1 mil, you have to pay 500k up front :)

I personally have stock in my company, it's granted for outstanding achievements, but I sell it as soon as I get the chance. You only live once, and it's now!
